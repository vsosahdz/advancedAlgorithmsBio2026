Diversity is mean distance to the swarm centroid. gbest collapses first because one good particle informs everyone in a single iteration; the ring passes news around the circle, so disagreement survives longer. Which is better depends on the landscape — that is the point, and it is why M2 asks you to measure it on your own instance from the same seeds rather than repeat the folklore.
Reading this honestly. Three panels from one seed is an anecdote, not a result. It shows the mechanism — information spreading at different rates — and nothing about which topology wins. Over 30 seeds on this landscape:

                 mean best   diversity   runs won
gbest           0.9269      0.0019      12/30
ring            0.9686      0.1867       5/30
von Neumann   0.9454      0.0553      13/30

Look at the last two columns. Ring has the best mean and wins the fewest runs. It rarely does badly, so its average is high; von Neumann wins more often but loses harder when it loses. Any single run — including the three above — could have told you either story. That contradiction is precisely why "which topology is better" needs 30 paired seeds, a dispersion measure and a test, and why the answer depends on whether you care about the typical run or the best one.
